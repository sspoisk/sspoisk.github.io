Fundamentals

Why Someone Else's Strategy Doesn't Work — and How to Find Your Own

A plain-English explanation of how markets actually work. No jargon, no magic. Just mechanics.

01 — Price Is Simply an Agreement

Bitcoin is worth $84,000 not because it "should" be worth that. Not because the exchange calculated it. And not because somewhere it says "fair value of BTC = $84k".

It's worth exactly that because right now one person is willing to sell for $84,000 — and another person is willing to buy. That's it. No magic beyond that.

Price is the point where a seller and buyer agreed. Not a "truth", not a "valuation", not "fairness". Just the moment two people reached an agreement.

When there are more buyers than sellers — price drifts up. More sellers than buyers — it drifts down. The market moves when this balance is disrupted. For any reason at all.

02 — Three Types of Players — and Where You Fit

Not everyone is equal in the market. Broadly speaking, there are three categories of participants, and each plays a different game.

🏦
Market Makers
Large structures that stand on both sides of the market simultaneously. Always ready to buy slightly lower and sell slightly higher. They profit from the spread — the difference between the bid and ask price.
They don't need to guess direction
🐋
Institutions
Hedge funds, large traders. They have enormous capital, but that's also their problem — they can't enter or exit quickly without moving the price against themselves. They need liquidity.
Strength = money. Weakness = slowness
👤
Retail — You
Small size, high speed. You can enter and exit in seconds without impacting the price. You can trade where the big player has nothing to gain — small-cap coins, fast moves.
Your only edge — speed and flexibility

Once you understand this, it becomes clear: playing "buy and hold like a fund" or "copy a professional's signals" means not using your own advantage. It's playing by someone else's rules on someone else's field.

03 — Why the Market Moves at All

Price movement always has a reason. Sometimes obvious, sometimes not. Three main sources:

News and events. Someone knew earlier — bought earlier. By the time the news appears on most people's screens, the smart money is already inside. That's why "trading the news" — in most cases — means buying from people who entered three hours ago.

Large order flow. A big player doesn't enter in a single trade. They accumulate a position gradually — over hours, sometimes days. Visible in volume: price slowly creeps up for no apparent reason, volume above normal. That's accumulation.

Technical levels and liquidity. This is the most interesting part. Huge numbers of traders place stops at the same spots — below round numbers, below local lows. The big player needs liquidity to build a position. So price often goes where stops are sitting, triggers them — and reverses.

The market is not random, but it's not predictable either. It has logic. But the logic is probabilistic — "usually this way", not "always this way".

04 — Why Someone Else's Strategy Doesn't Work

Here's where most people go wrong. People look for a "working strategy" as though it were a recipe — find it, reproduce it, everything works.

That's not how it works. Here's why.

A market inefficiency disappears as soon as enough people discover it.

Imagine: in 2012 someone noticed — "when RSI drops below 30, there's often a bounce". They traded it and made money. Then they wrote about it in a blog. Then a book came out. Then it made its way into every technical analysis course.

Now when RSI = 29 — thousands of people and hundreds of algorithms buy simultaneously. Price spikes before most of them can even enter. The edge is gone.

Even worse — paid signals and "strategies for sale":

1000 people subscribed to one channel. They receive one signal. Everyone enters simultaneously. The big player sees this — and sells into the order book. The buyers supported the price with their money, the smart money exited.

A public strategy is a strategy that has already lost its edge. Otherwise, it wouldn't have been published.

This doesn't mean technical analysis is useless. It means RSI on its own is not a strategy. It's a tool. The strategy is how you use it, in what context, with what filters, in what market regime.

05 — What a Real Edge Looks Like

In professional terms this is called an edge — an advantage over the market. Something you do that others don't do or can't.

Examples of real edges that work:

Adapting to market regime. Markets alternate between trending and ranging. Most strategies only work in one of them. If you can identify the regime and switch tactics accordingly — that's an edge. Not magic, not prediction. Just adaptation.

Specifics of a particular coin. Different coins behave differently. One has higher volatility in the mornings, another moves after BTC breaks out of a range. If you've observed enough — you see patterns others missed because they were watching something else.

Speed and discipline. Most traders break their own rules — entering without a signal, skipping stops, holding losing positions "just a little longer". A bot that rigidly follows rules already has an edge over 90% of the market.

An edge is not a secret formula. It's what you do consistently while others make mistakes.

06 — How to Find Your Own Strategy

You don't need to be a genius and you don't need years of experience. You need a method. Here's how it works in practice:

1
Observe and ask questions
Look at charts with the question "why". Why is there a reversal here? What happened before this rally? What was the volume at that moment? Don't look for the answer in a book — look for it on the chart.
2
Form a hypothesis
"It seems like after a sharp drop on high volume there's often a bounce within 2 hours". Specific. Measurable. Not "the market sometimes goes up".
3
Test it on historical data
Take a year of data. Count how many times it worked and how many times it didn't. If 55%+ — already interesting. If 40% — the hypothesis doesn't hold, find another one.
4
Add filters
Maybe it only works in a trend? Only on certain coins? Only during high volatility? Filters remove bad signals and improve accuracy.
5
Automate
Once the strategy is tested and understood — that's when you need a bot. Not before. A bot executes rules better than a human: no emotions, no fatigue, 24/7.

The core principle: a strategy must have a logic you can explain in words. "I buy when RSI is low" — that's not an explanation. "I buy a pullback in an uptrend because the big player will continue accumulating on the correction" — that's an explanation.
